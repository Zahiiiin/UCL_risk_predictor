# Choosing the target journal (facts read on 29 October 2026)

This note keeps the evidence behind the journal choice. Every number says where it came from. Nothing here is a promise of acceptance. No journal publishes the acceptance chance of a given paper, so the ranking below is a judgement from scope, length rules, cost and level, not a probability.

## 1. What "Q1" means (you must confirm which one your university uses)

There are two common systems and they can disagree for the same journal.

- Scopus / SCImago (SJR): quartiles are computed inside a subject category from the SJR score. I read the 2025 ranking directly from SCImago.
- Web of Science (Journal Citation Reports, JIF): quartiles come from the impact factor inside a Web of Science category. I could not open Clarivate's site. One aggregator (Peeref) lists several of these journals as Q1 in "Nuclear Science & Technology", but I could not confirm this with Clarivate. Treat it as unverified and check in Journal Citation Reports through your university library.

## 6. Ranking, with the reasons

1. Progress in Nuclear Energy (primary). The scope names plant technical aspects and safety and welcomes fleet-level topics. It asks for medium to long papers, which suits a paper of this size. Q1 on SCImago 2025 (rank 15). A free subscription route exists.
2. Annals of Nuclear Energy (first backup). Same level and same cost route, no length limit. The scope is more tied to reactor physics and engineering, so a fleet-statistics paper is a little less central. Its submission-to-acceptance time is shorter.
3. Nuclear Engineering and Design (second backup). Q1 on SCImago 2025 but at rank 20, close to the edge. The scope is design and safety of reactors, which fits the design-feature part less well than the operations part.
4. Reliability Engineering and System Safety (reach option). Unplanned capability loss is a reliability measure, so the topic fits, and the level is much higher (impact factor 13.7). The reported acceptance rate is 20 percent, and it expects clear problem-solving value. This paper's honest result is a modest gain over simple rules, so it is the hardest target. Use it first only if your supervisor believes the contribution is strong enough.
5. Nuclear Engineering and Technology is not recommended as first choice: the 10-page guideline conflicts with a long paper, pages beyond 8 are charged, and its Q1 status is uncertain.

If the first journal rejects the paper, the same Word file with its Elsevier front matter can go to the next one. Only the abstract length (250 words for the first three, 200 for the last) and the reference style need changing.

## 7. What this means for the manuscript

- Build the front matter for Elsevier: abstract up to 200 words (the lowest limit I found, so it fits every journal on the list), 3 to 5 highlights of at most 85 characters each, up to 6 keywords (the lowest limit among them).
- Add a data availability statement, a generative AI declaration section before the references, CRediT author contributions, a funding statement and a competing interests statement. You add the author names.
- Deposit the data and code in a persistent repository (for example a DOI-issuing archive), not only a GitHub link. The repository must be public for this.
- References: numbered is fine at submission for all four journals (the journal applies its own style at proof).
